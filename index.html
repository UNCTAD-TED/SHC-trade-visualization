Methodology & Data Sources
Global Second-Hand Clothes Trade Monitor — Technical Notes

Data Source

Database
BACI International Trade Database (CEPII), derived from UN Comtrade mirror data. Harmonized bilateral trade flows at the HS 6-digit level.
Product coverage
HS 6309 — Worn clothing and other worn articles: worn clothing, clothing accessories, blankets, household linen, furnishing articles, footwear and headgear showing signs of appreciable wear, presented in bulk, bales, sacks or similar packings. Rags (HS 6310) are not included.
Years available
2015–2024 (provisional for the most recent year)
Value unit
Current US dollars (FOB), displayed as $K / $M / $B.
Weight unit
Kilograms (BACI harmonized export weight), displayed in metric tonnes (t / kt / Mt).
Metric toggle
The Value / Weight switch in the toolbar recomputes every figure on the map, legend, KPIs and country panels from the selected measure. Net flow direction, thresholds and rankings are all derived from whichever metric is active, so the direction of a net flow can differ between value and weight.
Not shown
Trade recorded as “Other Asia, nes” and with special partner areas (bunkers, free zones, special categories) is not shown.

Flow Direction Classification

Economies are classified as North (developed) or South (developing, including least developed) using the UNCTAD development status classification, consistent with UNCTAD flagship publications.

N→S
Net flow from a developed economy to a developing economy
S→N
Net flow from a developing economy to a developed economy
S→S
Both economies are developing (including least developed)
N→N
Both economies are developed

Trade Flow Calculation

Net bilateral flow
For each pair of economies (A, B), the net flow is A’s exports to B minus B’s exports to A. The line runs from the net exporter to the net importer, and its size reflects the absolute net amount. Trade in both directions therefore partly offsets; the gross flows in each direction are shown in the corridor history and in the CSV downloads.
Line width
Square-root scale of the net flow, so that large flows do not hide small ones. Flows above the 98th percentile of those shown are drawn at the maximum width. Larger flows are also drawn more opaque.
Line curvature
All lines curve in a consistent clockwise direction (SVG sweep-flag = 1). Eastward flows bend northward and westward flows bend southward, a secondary cue for direction that complements the colour.
Circles
Colour shows the economy’s net balance (exports minus imports) over all flows in the current region or selection, before the display threshold: blue for net exporters, red for net importers. Size shows the total of the flows drawn for that economy.

What the Map Shows

To avoid visual overload, the map draws only the largest net flows. The totals in the legend (“Shown” and “Total”) tell how much of the trade in the current region or selection is on the map.

Auto (default)
At most 40 lines. A minimum flow applies first: $10 M in the global view, $1 M for a region; with countries selected, $10 K (1–3 countries), $100 K (4–10), $500 K (11–30) or $1 M (31 or more). If more than 40 flows clear it, the 40 largest are drawn. In weight mode the same steps apply in tonnes (10 kt, 1 kt, 10 t, 100 t, 500 t, 1 kt).
Fixed thresholds
$10 M to $10 K (10 kt to 10 t in weight mode): every flow at or above the chosen value is drawn.
Lines
Draws only the N largest flows. With Auto it replaces the 40-line limit; with a fixed threshold both conditions apply. The N largest are taken before the flow-direction boxes, so hiding a direction can leave fewer than N lines.
Regions
A region shows only flows between economies of that region.
Connect
Drawn connections are shown whatever their size; the region, threshold and Lines settings do not apply to them.
Country panel
Gross trade is the economy’s exports plus imports with all partners; net balance is exports minus imports. Partner lists, concentration, trade split, fingerprint and flow composition use all of the economy’s net flows, never the display threshold, Lines limit or flow-direction boxes; when a region is selected they are limited to partners in that region.

Partner Concentration (HHI)

Shown in the country panel. Measures how much an economy’s trade is spread across partners or concentrated on a few — does it trade with everyone, or depend heavily on one partner?

Index used
Herfindahl–Hirschman Index (HHI): the sum of squared partner shares, scaled to 0–10,000. A score of 10,000 means all trade is with a single partner; a score near 0 means trade is spread evenly across many partners.
Formula
HHI = ∑(sharei²) × 10,000, where sharei is partner i’s share of the economy’s net bilateral flows. Export and import partners are counted together, in the selected measure (value or weight).
Interpretation
■ Diversified (below 2,000): trade spread across many partners
■ Moderate (2,000 to below 4,000): some partners dominate but diversification remains
■ Highly concentrated (4,000 and above): trade heavily dependent on one or two partners
Threshold note
These bands are specific to this tool and indicative. They are more lenient than the thresholds used in competition law (for example, the 2023 United States Merger Guidelines treat markets above 1,800 as highly concentrated).
Data scope
All of the economy’s net bilateral flows for the selected year, regardless of the display threshold, Lines limit and flow-direction boxes; limited to partners in the region when a region is selected.

Maritime Route Visualization

Coverage
Sea routes can be switched on in the country panel (“Sea Route”, experimental). They are drawn only for flows between different UNCTAD regions (Africa, Americas, Asia, Europe, Oceania); flows within a region keep their curved line to avoid clutter from short routes.
Algorithm
Routes are computed with the Eurostat searoute algorithm over a navigable sea graph.
Port selection
Each economy is routed through one representative port, chosen mainly as its highest-ranked port in the UNCTAD Port Liner Shipping Connectivity Index (PLSCI), with some ports set manually. Landlocked economies are routed through a gateway port of a neighbouring country. For the United States, Canada and Australia, a Pacific-coast port is used when the partner lies east of 60°E and an Atlantic- or Indian-Ocean-coast port otherwise.
Disclaimer
Routes are indicative only. They do not represent actual tracked HS 6309 cargo paths, may not reflect the mode of transport used, and should not be used for navigation.
